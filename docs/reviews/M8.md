## What M8 set out to do, and what it did

The owner chose the campaign on 2026-09-21: **continue the reinforcement learning of a policy
trained elsewhere**, ahead of the editor. The spec's claim was a second proof of §8's thesis —
M5 did it for imitation (LeRobot ACT); plan S does it for a PPO MLP and a reward — plus one
step further, continuation in our simulation. Measured, on the oracle server (Linux, RTX 4090,
16-core CPU) unless said otherwise:

- **Episode boundaries (R1).** `begin_episode` empties the ring; `events.json`'s `tick` counts
  from the episode (the collector's stream-2 tick too); the `(cell, episode)` partition ships.
  On the committed demo documents `--jobs 1`, `2` and `4` are byte-identical in `report.json`,
  `events.json`, every `.estraj` and 7,204 frames; the nominal suite (16 episodes) runs in
  **60.5 s at `--jobs 4` against 125.7 s at `--jobs 1` (48 %)**, reproducing T8's unshipped
  measurement to 0.2 %. Re-measured under the new semantics, U3's held-out `report.json` is
  **byte-identical** (0.5625) and the expert gate unchanged (1.0 / 0.0432): the window feeds one
  watchdog that trips above `max_frac = 0.9`, and neither run was near it — T8's `v14` (0.9998)
  was. The numbers §28.11 expected to move did not move, and the record says why.
- **The live table (R12/R16).** Rows are `(stage, cell)`; a viewer that attaches mid-episode gets
  a row from the next end event, marked "joined late"; E4's and E7's oracles unchanged.
- **The node set (S2a).** `StateEncoder{Mlp}` declares `activation` (relu / elu / swish / tanh)
  and `activate_output`; `PolicyHead` declares `squash` (none / tanh); absent = default = today's
  canonical bytes, so no committed `learning_hash` or `lowering_hash` moved; 16 activation ×
  output × squash combinations bitwise against a torch reference.
- **The source policy (S2c).** No SO-101 policy exists upstream, so one was trained with the
  upstream stack on our scene: brax 0.14.2 / Playground 0.2.0 / jax 0.9.0 (0.10+ removed an API
  brax calls). The committed scene loads in MJX unmodified and is untrainable (1,054 contacts,
  574 env-steps/s, `CG` diverges to NaN); the derived `so101_reach_mjx.xml` adds 35
  `<contact><exclude>` rows and nothing else (10,620 env-steps/s). 2 M steps in 540 s, success
  **1.00** in the derived scene, **bitwise reproducible across four runs**; and **0.00 on the
  committed scene** — the policy reaches the cube through the table (S-5).
- **The rollout binding (S4a).** `es_native.Rollout` steps `Env` + one `SafetyPlane` and one
  `CpuPlan` per env from Python through `es-eval`'s own capture; 100 steps × 2 envs bitwise
  against a hand-rolled loop, golden generated on Windows and reproduced on Linux.
- **Starting from a policy (S1).** `[init] policy`, `init.lock` entering `training_hash`,
  `steps = 0` as "checkpoint immediately"; U3's checkpoint re-packed at 0 steps is bitwise U3
  (126 tensors copied) with the same bundle `policy_hash` and a different run identity.
- **The importer (S2b).** `import_rl.py` (brax / rsl_rl ≥ 5 and < 5 / rl_games) → `safetensors` +
  `import.json`; `es policy import-rl` → the two documents, the bundle and the mapping report
  through `adapter-so101.toml`. On S2c's checkpoint: the numpy→torch reconstruction vs our
  runtime **bitwise** (1,000 rows), our runtime vs JAX **9.7e-7** (§8.9 tier 4, ≤ 1e-5); rsl_rl
  5.5.1 and rl_games 1.6.5 native actors **bitwise**. Three synthetic fixtures make the CI test
  Python-free.
- **The trainer (S4b) and the cone it needed (S4d, S4e).** `[rl]` in the recipe, `train_ppo.py`
  (Gaussian around the module's actuator-unit output, training-only `log_std`, value MLP, GAE,
  clipped objective), two runs bitwise on the CPU backend, iteration 0 from `[init]` equal to
  the import tensor for tensor. S4b found the reach reward was spellable and not executable:
  S4d put `Expr::Sqrt` (an IEEE basic operation, §6.6), `Source::Xpos` lanes and `Norm{L2}` into
  the cone with the demo task's lowering pinned unmoved; S4e put `JointState.quantity` and
  `Capture::Qvel / JointsVel / BodyPose` into the capture with every committed hash pinned
  unmoved. The reach task then trains: held-out `success_rate` 0.0 / 0.19 / 0.25 / 0.44 /
  **0.5625** / 0.50 / 0.31 / 0.31 at 1k … 10k iterations — the 0.8 target **not** reached, the
  peak at 4,000, the run unlearning past it while rollout entropy climbs 4.87 → 6.06 (S-3).
- **Before and after continuation (S4c).** One Evaluation IR (`evaluation-reach.toml`, 16 held-out
  seeds) over every row; three training seeds per training row, 4,000 PPO iterations each:

  | row | graph, init | held-out `success_rate` (mean; min / max) |

  **Continuation did nothing, and the mechanism is measured:** after 4,000 iterations the
  continued actor's six tensors are bit-identical to the imported ones on all three seeds (one
  `policy_hash 29cfcd15…` across seeds); on the committed observations the smallest pre-squash
  value over 50 ticks is 23.7 (largest 340.3), where f32 `tanh'` is exactly `0.0` — the squash is
  saturated, the actor's gradient is zero, and only `log_std` moves (entropy 5.53 → 13.55) while
  the critic trains normally. The same graph from random init learns (0.25 on its best seed);
  the committed 64×64 graph is the best of the three at this budget, and S4e's single seed sits
  at the top of its spread. Two deviations the note records: the straight import is refused by
  `es eval run` (`XIR-040` — the importer emits brax's `Normalize{MeanStd}`, the committed reach
  Observation IR is the identity, and the Evaluation IR pins the observation hash), so brax's
  normalizer was folded into the first Dense (an exact rewrite, checked at 2.6e-5 on the 1,000-row
  oracle — above tier (b) only because `1/std` ≈ 5,600 on the cube-z channel) and re-imported
  under the committed Observation IR bit for bit; and the eight training runs ran two at a time
  (wall-clock only).

## Findings

### Blocker

None.

### Should-fix

- **S-1 (R1).** `shard_thread_env`'s `cores/N` cap changes torch's CPU numerics: `--jobs 8`
  (cap 2) differs from `--jobs 1` at `nominal-00` tick 324 — episode 0, where `seek_episode` is a
  no-op — and `--jobs 1` with `OMP_NUM_THREADS=2` reproduces the `--jobs 8` bytes. The worker
  thread count is not in `execution_hash`; row U0's committed numbers are the cap-2 numbers.
  Predates this milestone (M5/V5); three options in `evaluation-execution.md` 2.7 (pin the pool,
  hash the pool, or declare tier 3 above the pool); the help text's byte-identity claim was
  narrowed to what is true.
- **S-2 (S4d, pre-existing).** `crates/es-assets/src/mjcf/orient.rs:56` converts `euler=` with
  `f64::sin_cos` — platform libm — so `SceneDesc::scene_hash()` differs between Windows and
  Linux for identical XML (the demo task's `scene_hash` moves; `asset_hash` does not). A
  `DET-010` violation on the asset path feeding the §5.3 chain. Fixing it moves every scene hash.
- **S-3 (S4b, S4e).** The plane clamps **every** rollout tick (`envelope_violation_rate` and
  `executed_ne_sampled_rate` 1.00 in every iteration of every run, demo and reach alike): a
  position-target Gaussian with `log_std = −0.5` around a 50 Hz policy violates the per-tick
  velocity/acceleration bounds structurally, so every gradient is computed from the
  log-probability of an action the env never executed. This is `rl-continuation.md` open
  question 2 made concrete, and the likeliest cause of the reach run's decay after its peak.
  Candidates, in the order the S4e table suggests: train on the executed action (the plane as
  part of the environment), an entropy coefficient and a learning-rate schedule the RL route
  currently leaves constant, and a narrower initial `log_std`.
- **S-4 (S4e).** `CpuPlan` names every observation input `Home::Input(source_id)`, so two
  channels on one joint id collapse to one buffer; `joint_vel` therefore names `shoulder_pan`
  and `input_sources` refuses the pair by name. The fix is `es-compile`'s (key by `(id,
  quantity)`), not this milestone's.
- **S-5 (S2c).** The source policy trained in the contact-stripped derived scene does not
  transfer: 0.00 with the arm-vs-world rows restored, 0.00 on the committed scene, no
  divergence — it reaches the cube through the table and itself. The sim-to-sim gap is the
  scene's contact set, not the network, and it is the honest number S4c starts from.
- **S-6 (S2b).** brax's running normalizer leaves three observation channels at a 1e-4…1e-6 std
  floor (cube z, two quaternion components), so the packet's `U(−1, 1)` oracle draw saturates
  `tanh` on 80 % of rows; tier (b) is measured on `obs_scaled` and the raw draw recorded
  (2.3e-5). Also: the stored JAX actions are computed at `highest` matmul precision — TF32
  differs by 1.5e-3, 100× the tier.
- **S-7 (S4a).** `SafetyPlane::from_ir` requires `H = action.horizon`, so `Rollout<6, 16>` on the
  demo deployment sends one filled row of a 16-row chunk per tick under a fresh `seq`. It is
  "horizon 1" in effect and the RL deployment documents declare `horizon = 1`; a plane that
  accepts a shorter chunk would remove the fiction.
- **S-8 (pre-existing, M7 S-10).** `quadruped_eval_run_names_the_observation_gap` still fails on
  a box with `ES_PYTHON` set (the placeholder weights are refused before the observation-gap
  refusal); green in CI only because CI has no torch.
- **S-9 (R12, S1, S4b, small).** `LiveRun::cell`'s bare-name fallback is a linear scan with a
  `ponytail:` note; `editor-shell.md:645` still names `record_cell` (now `cell_results`);
  `train_act.py`'s summary prints absolute paths (S4b's `train_ppo.py` fixed its own copy of that
  because it broke oracle 2 — the same latent hash instability sits in `train_act.py`); the
  `Cargo.lock` of M5 R7 is still untracked.
- **S-10 (R1, T8).** `~/artifacts/plan-v/v14/trained-20000.esb` no longer judges the committed
  documents (`XIR-040`); T8's wall-clock table and R1's were taken with different bundles (U0's
  for R1), which is why R1's `--jobs 1` (125.7 s) is not T8's (121.6 s).
- **S-11 (M7 addendum, read this milestone).** U4 — the same policy, data and recipe on
  path-traced observations — scores 0 of 16 held-out and 1 of 16 on its own training seeds
  against U3's 0.5625 on rasterized frames. R13 (the 64 spp grain) is the first suspect; the
  measurement belongs to the M7 addendum and is recorded there.

- **S-12 (S4c).** An imported policy carries its own Observation IR (the source's normalizer),
  and the Evaluation IR pins one `observation_hash` (M7/R5's rule), so a straight import cannot
  be scored by its task's committed Evaluation IR: S4c had to fold the normalizer into the first
  Dense. §7.4 says one `task_hash` may carry several Observation IRs; the evaluator does not
  yet honour that. Either the importer folds a `MeanStd` normalizer by default (exact, but it
  moves the tier-(b) error where `1/std` is large), or `es eval run` accepts a declared
  Observation IR sibling of the same `task_hash` and records both hashes.
- **S-13 (S4c).** A saturated squash is a dead actor: `tanh'` is exactly `0.0` in f32 past
  |x| ≈ 9.0, and the imported policy sits at 23–340 on the committed observations, so PPO from
  it cannot move a weight. `train_ppo.py` should detect and report a zero actor-gradient norm
  (and a `log_std` that runs away while the actor does not move) in its first iteration rather
  than spend 4,000 of them; the mapping report should warn when a source normalizer's `1/std`
  puts an in-distribution observation into the saturated region.

### Human decision

- **The plane in RL rollouts (S-3).** Training on the executed action makes the plane part of
  the environment and the importer's Gaussian irrelevant to the gradient; training on the
  sample keeps the textbook estimator and clamps every tick. The spec (§13.4) chose the
  sample and recorded the rate; S-3 says the rate is 1.00. The owner chooses whether the next
  packet changes the estimator, the envelope (a Deployment IR decision, INV-12: widen, never
  disable), or the action space (delta actions, §8.5 — the `es-ir` split packet first).
- **The worker thread pool (S-1).** Pinning it makes `--jobs N` bitwise across N at the cost of
  throughput on a big box; hashing it makes every row honest and moves nothing; declaring the
  policy runtime tier 3 above the pool says the same in the spec.
- **`scene_hash` across platforms (S-2).** The fix (`es-math::approx` or an integer-exact
  euler→quat) moves every scene hash and therefore every committed `task_hash`; like R5/R6 of
  M7 it is a re-collect-and-retrain decision.
- **Isaac as the second source.** The parsers and native fixtures exist (rsl_rl, rl_games,
  bitwise); a real Isaac Lab policy needs a scene through `es-usd` and the same contact
  question S-5 raised.
- **The line budget.** `es`, `es-data`, `es-ir` are over 6,000; §1.5 asks for split packets.
- **Where continuation goes next (S4c, S-5, S-13).** The negative result has two causes and both
  are the source's: a policy trained in a contact-stripped scene, and a normalizer that saturates
  its own head on the committed observations. The next source policy has to be trained on the
  committed contact set (MJX with the Newton solver at ≈ 600 env-steps/s, or another trainer)
  or on a scene whose contact set is declared equal to ours; only then does "continue" mean
  fine-tune rather than re-learn. The owner chooses whether that is the next campaign, or
  whether the from-scratch trainer (0.4167 at 4,000 iterations, three seeds) is the product
  and continuation waits for a real external policy on a matching scene.

## Verdict

**M8: passed as engineering; the campaign's product claim is not demonstrated, and the
record says exactly why.** Every packet's oracles hold on the committed documents: the episode
boundary decision bought the 2× evaluation with no committed number moved; a PPO actor from
brax, rsl_rl or rl_games becomes a bundle that reproduces its source (bitwise where the
frameworks share torch, 9.7e-7 against JAX); a recipe starts from any policy and records what it
copied; PPO runs through our env with the plane on, bitwise on the CPU backend, and learns the
reach task from scratch to 0.42 (three seeds) at the measured 4,000-iteration peak. What did not
happen is the sentence the owner asked for: the imported policy scores 0.00 in our simulation and
continuation leaves it at 0.00 — not because the trainer fails but because the source policy is
dead on arrival here, trained through a table our scene has and saturated on observations our
scene produces (S-5, S-13). That is the honest sim-to-sim number the campaign was designed to
produce, and it points at the source, not at the runtime. The four rules held; `es-safety`
changed by one line; the three findings that outlive the milestone — the plane clamping every
RL tick (S-3), the worker pool changing CPU numerics (S-1), and `scene_hash` depending on libm
(S-2) — are the owner's decisions and the next ladder's first rows.
