## What plan X set out to do, and what it did

The owner asked on 2026-09-23 for sim-to-sim (with the I/O of Isaac- and MuJoCo-trained policies matched), domain randomization
for vision RL, and PT noise handled by the per-tick seed and SVGF. The owner decided that Isaac Sim becomes a backend, that visual
randomization covers all four groups, that vision RL trains on `Pt`, and, on 2026-09-24, that the X7 rerun covers the four
pixel-only rows at 4 spp. Measured on the oracle server (Linux, RTX 4090) and the Windows RTX 3060 box:

- **One dispatch, four engines (X1, I0, I1, R1).**
  - `es eval run`, `es loop collect` and `es_native.Rollout` take `--backend` over `mujoco-cpu`, `mjwarp` and `physx` (Isaac Sim 5.1,
    PhysX 107.3, CPU or GPU pipeline). `newton` is refused by its mapping report.
  - MuJoCo and MJWarp track to 1e-7 until contact. MJWarp's elliptic cone is allowed as tier 2 after the owner relaxed spec 17.2.
  - PhysX CPU and GPU reruns are bitwise. MJWarp reruns are not.
- **Adapter v2 (X2).**
  - What it does: an Isaac Lab or Playground policy's joint names, default pose, per-term scales, action offset/scale and
    `last_action` compile into the bundle, with `ObsSource::PreviousAction` as the one new Task IR variant.
  - Accuracy: 1.2e-7 against the rsl_rl reference and 7.6e-8 against Playground.
  - What it refuses: observation clipping has no Clamp node and is refused by name.
- **Model parameters reach physics (X4).** `set_params` reproduces a direct `MjModel` edit bitwise, Linux == Windows. MJWarp does
  this through per-world model rows.
- **Pixels in the RL loop (X3, X3b).**
  - `Rollout` renders each env, and its frame equals the collector's bitwise.
  - `EnvBatchRenderer` puts N envs in one dispatch, with one world-space BVH per env and tiles bitwise equal to single renders.
  - Cost on the 4090 at 64 spp: per-env time drops from 62 to 11.4 ms at N = 16.
  - Frames past 2^24 sample-bounces are traced in bands of rows, so the Windows driver does not kill them.
- **Visual randomization (X5, R2).**
  - Targets: `light.{intensity,direction,color,ambient,radiance,sky}`, `geom.<n>.rgba`, `camera.<n>.pose.*` and `camera.<n>.fov`.
  - `Pt` gains a directional light (`light.radiance`). A drawn fov moves the episode's intrinsics.
  - After R2, collector, evaluator and `Rollout` render one draw as one frame.
  - The two remaining `Cross` builtins measured exact. The camera's `Cross` had fused on one driver and was rewritten (X5).
- **PT noise (X6, U6).**
  - `render.svgf` is a declared field, 0 ULP GPU == CPU, and costs +3.9 ms/frame.
  - On the demo's `Pt` row at two training seeds each, SVGF does not move held-out success by more than the seed spread:
    U5 0.25 / 0.375, U6 0.375 / 0.0625.
  - SVGF does repeatably raise the final loss: 0.0047 against 0.0021 on both seeds.
- **Sim-to-sim, measured (I3).**
  - Three SO-101 reach policies trained in Isaac Lab average 0.76 on Isaac's own held-out resets. Imported through adapter v2, they
    average 0.52 on `physx` CPU, 0.56 on `physx` GPU, 0.56 on `mujoco-cpu` and 0.58 / 0.46 on two `mjwarp` runs.
  - A0, trained here, falls from 0.42 on `mujoco-cpu` to 0.19 / 0.08 on `physx`.
  - Attribution found no single cause:
    - the evaluator's one-tick action latency costs Isaac's own policies 0.15;
    - removing joint damping zeroes every Isaac policy on `physx`;
    - frictionloss is the largest mover on MuJoCo.
- **Vision RL on `Pt` (X7, R3).**
  - R3 made the learner run on CUDA with the CPU bytes unmoved, cutting the update from 18.9 to 0.42 s per iteration.
  - The pixel-only rows ran with the cube's pose removed from the state: `Pt` and `Rs`, randomization on and off, 3 seeds, 4,000
    iterations, `Pt` at 4 spp with SVGF off by the pre-written rule.
  - **All twelve policies score 0.00 on every held-out suite.** The training return moved less than the seed spread and
    entropy rose in every run.
  - Cost: `Pt` renders at 2.0–3.3× the rasterizer's cost per iteration, which makes a run 3.4–4.1 h against 2.8 h.
  - Cross-render transfer could not be measured, because XIR-040 refuses it (S-2).
- **The hash chain's scene hole (R4).** `Env::new` now refuses a scene whose content hash the Task IR does not pin. I3 had run four
  edited scenes under the committed `execution_hash`. `es task generate --scene` now writes the loaded scene's hash, and all 14
  committed task documents already matched their scenes.

## Findings

### Blocker

None.

### Should-fix

- **S-1 (I3, X7; M10 S-3, still open).** `envelope_violation_rate` is 1.00 in every I3 evaluation cell and in every X7
  training iteration, including the Isaac policies that reach 0.69–0.94 under it. The envelope clamps something on every step of
  every learning or imported policy. For the imported ones it sits beside the one-tick latency and the damping gap, and it is part of
  the 0.76 → 0.52 drop. For pixel-only PPO it is the first suspect for the flat return. The lever the owner has deferred since M8 now
  blocks both halves of this campaign.
- **S-2 (X7).** A cross-render evaluation cannot be expressed. The sensor's `render` block is in the Task IR, so `Pt` and `Rs`
  are different tasks to `task_hash`, and an Evaluation IR for one refuses a bundle of the other (XIR-040, 12 of 12). The refusal is
  correct under §10.4 as written, so the question "which renderer transfers" needs a design decision, not a workaround. For example:
  the render block becomes an evaluation-side condition, or a declared render override that enters `evaluation_hash`.
- **S-3 (I3).** The evaluator applies a policy's action one tick late (`es eval run`), while Isaac Lab trains at zero latency.
  Isaac's own policies lose 0.15 to that tick alone. Neither the adapter nor the Deployment IR can declare the source's latency, so an
  imported policy is always scored under a condition it was not trained under.
- **S-4 (X1, I3).** MJWarp is not run-to-run reproducible under one `execution_hash`: reach A0 0.5625 vs 0.4375 / 0.4375, and Isaac
  seed 0 0.9375 / 0.8125. Tier 2 permits it, but two different numbers carry the same hash, and the report does not say which tier
  produced it.
- **S-5 (X1).** es-env writes a zero quaternion for a free joint at reset. MuJoCo normalizes it, while MJWarp and PhysX read a half
  turn. Both adapters normalize now. Fixing it at the source would move every committed `.estraj` tick-0 row, so it is left as it is.
  The next backend has to know.
- **S-6 (R2).** Under `--expert` on the reach deployment, collector and evaluator states part at tick 2 (qpos ~1e-5), while the
  demo deployment agrees to the last tick. The cause is open. The frame parity oracle compares only the ticks where the states agree.
- **S-7 (M10 S-1, still open).** `quadruped_eval_run_names_the_observation_gap` still fails under `ES_PYTHON` and stops
  `xtask ci`'s fail-fast test stage. Read again this time: a well-formed empty safetensors is not enough, because the runtime checks
  the checkpoint against the lowered graph (8 tensors missing) before the observation gap. The fixture needs tensors of the lowered
  shapes, or the test must pin the checkpoint refusal instead.
- **S-8 (X3b).** On the 4090, `cargo test -p es-render --test render` stalls with about 15 concurrent `VkDevice`s. It passes with
  `--test-threads=1` in 67 s. Every server run uses that flag; the cause (driver limit, or a new test) is not known.

## Verdict

**M11: passed, with one measured negative.**

What was built:
- Sim-to-sim is built end to end. A policy runs closed-loop on four engines, and the engine is a hash-chain condition.
- An Isaac Lab policy's I/O conventions compile into the bundle exactly, with nothing framework-specific at inference.
- Every visual and physical randomization target reaches the frame or the physics bitwise, wherever the frame is rendered.
- PT noise is two declared fields. The hash chain lost a hole: a run can no longer claim a scene it did not load.

What was measured:
- **Sim-to-sim is possible and lossy.** Isaac's 0.76 becomes about 0.55 here. The gap is spread across latency, joint damping
  and friction modelling, with the envelope clamping throughout.
- **Per-tick seeding helps and SVGF does not.** The per-tick seed was the M10 result. SVGF's effect is inside two training seeds.
- **Vision RL on pixels does not learn under today's recipe and envelope.** On `Pt` or `Rs`, with randomization on or off, three
  seeds each, it scores 0.00. The machinery works, and the RL lever is the owner's decision (S-1).

### Findings from the follow-ups

- **S-9 (R10): no ImageNet normalization in front of a pretrained backbone.** The `-pix` image chain
  ends with `Normalize { Range 0..1 }`, and `lower/torch.rs` feeds that tensor straight into
  `_frozen_backbone`. A frozen ImageNet ResNet18 therefore reads inputs its FrozenBatchNorm
  statistics were not fitted to. The demo's fine-tuned `learning-pretrained.toml` trained around
  this; a frozen encoder cannot. Where the ImageNet mean and std belong is an IR decision. It could
  be an Observation IR `Normalize` whose unit the `VisionEncoder` accepts, or a property of
  `pretrained = true` in the lowering. Until it is made, the frozen-encoder arm cannot be measured.
- **S-10 (R10, R11): the actor saturates again late.** The best R11 actors have 35–88 % of \|z\| > 3
  at 4,000 iterations. S4c (import), R10 and R11 are one failure: a squashed Gaussian mean with a
  zero-gradient corner and an envelope that clamps the corner every step. It sits beside S-1.
- **Nit, fixed (`f824bc5`):** `Schedule::warmup` was documented as optimizer steps. It counts
  `[run] steps`' unit, which is iterations on the `[rl]` route.
- **Nit:** `es train` refuses `warmup >= steps`, so a short probe of a long schedule has to run
  past the ramp (R11 ran 101 iterations and read 1–20).
- **Nit:** `mujoco-cpu` declares determinism tier 3 in its capabilities while its CPU reruns are
  bitwise. R9 prints the declared tier. Whether the declaration should say tier 1 is a spec reading.
